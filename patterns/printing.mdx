With your patterns arranged in the Pattern Layout editor, printing is the final step before you start cutting and building. Getting the print settings right is critical — a pattern printed at the wrong scale produces pieces that simply will not fit your body, no matter how accurate your avatar measurements were.

## Starting the Print Dialog

You can open the print dialog in two ways:

- Navigate to **File → Print** from the main menu
- Use the **Print** button directly in the Pattern Layout toolbar

Either route opens the same print dialog, where you configure paper size, scale, and page range before sending the job to your printer.

## Setting Paper Size

In the print dialog, confirm that the paper size matches what is loaded in your printer. Common options include:

- **A4** (210 × 297 mm) — standard in most of the world
- **Letter** (8.5 × 11 in) — standard in North America
- **Tabloid / A3** (11 × 17 in / 297 × 420 mm) — useful for large pieces that would otherwise be split across many Letter or A4 sheets

If you change paper size here, make sure your Pattern Layout page size matches — otherwise pieces may shift or be clipped at the page edges.

## Scale Settings

<Warning>
  Never use your printer's "Fit to Page" or "Scale to fit" option when printing templates. Fitting the page to the printable area shrinks your patterns to fill the paper, making every piece the wrong size for your body. Always print at **100% / actual size / no scaling**, and confirm your printer driver is not applying any automatic scaling of its own.
</Warning>

In Armorsmith's print dialog, ensure the scale is set to **Print to scale** (or 100%). This guarantees that the measurements encoded in the pattern — derived from your avatar — are reproduced accurately on paper.

## Printing on the Right Media

While ordinary printer paper works, consider printing on **cardstock** (65–110 lb / ~175–300 gsm). Cardstock templates:

- Hold up better during the cutting and tracing process
- Do not tear when you trace around them multiple times
- Lie flat on foam or fabric without curling under your marker or stylus

If your printer cannot handle cardstock, printing on standard paper and gluing or spray-mounting the sheets to a sheet of cardstock is a reliable workaround.

## Multi-Page Print Workflow

For large costume sections, your patterns may span many pages. Follow this workflow to keep things organized:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Print page one first">
    Send only the first page to the printer to verify scale before committing to the full print run.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify scale with a ruler">
    Most pattern layouts include a scale reference bar, or you can measure a known dimension — such as the width of a piece you measured on your body. Place a ruler on the printed page and confirm the measurement matches. If it does not, revisit your printer's scaling settings and reprint.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Print remaining pages">
    Once you have confirmed the scale is correct, print the remaining pages. Use your printer's page range field to print in batches if needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Label printed pages">
    As pages come out of the printer, note the page number on the back with a pencil so you can keep them in order during assembly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Print a spare copy of any pages containing small or intricate pieces. Having a backup means a miscut does not send you back to the printer mid-build.
</Tip>

With your pages printed and scale verified, you are ready to cut out the pieces and begin transferring them to your build material.
