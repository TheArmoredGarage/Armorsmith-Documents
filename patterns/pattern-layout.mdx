After generating templates, all of your unfolded 2D pieces land in the Pattern Layout editor — a canvas that represents one or more printable pages, much like a cutting mat laid out on a table. This is where you take control of how your patterns are arranged before they hit paper. Efficient layout means less paper waste, easier cutting, and a smoother assembly experience.

## What the Pattern Layout Editor Shows

The Pattern Layout editor displays your flat, unfolded pattern pieces placed on a white page canvas. Each page corresponds to one sheet of paper at the size you have configured. The pieces themselves show fold lines, cut lines, and edge labels that guide assembly. A panel or toolbar lets you manage pages and adjust settings.

## Navigating the Layout

Use the following controls to move around the canvas:

- **Pan** — Click and drag on an empty area of the canvas (or use the middle mouse button) to scroll around the page.
- **Zoom** — Use the scroll wheel to zoom in and out. Zooming in helps you inspect edge labels and fine details; zooming out gives you a full overview of piece placement.

## Arranging Pattern Pieces

Organizing your pieces well before printing saves paper and makes the cut-out process far less confusing.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Drag and drop pieces into position">
    Click any pattern piece to select it, then drag it to a new position on the canvas. Pieces can be placed anywhere on the current page, or dragged to a different page if you have multiple pages open.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rotate pieces to fit more efficiently">
    With a piece selected, use the rotate control to spin it. Rotating odd-shaped pieces to nest them together — fitting a concave piece alongside a convex one, for example — can significantly reduce the number of pages you need to print.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Distribute pieces across multiple pages">
    If your pieces do not all fit on a single page, move overflow pieces to additional pages. Keep related pieces grouped when possible to make assembly easier.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Organize your pages by body section — for example, keep all helmet pieces on one page (or a dedicated set of pages), all chest pieces together, and so on. When you sit down to cut and assemble, you can work through one section at a time without hunting across dozens of mixed pages.
</Tip>

## Understanding Piece Labels and Edge Numbers

Every piece in the Pattern Layout is labeled so you know exactly how the flat cut-outs reconnect into the 3D shape during assembly.

<Accordion title="How edge labels work">
  Each edge of a pattern piece carries a number or letter. When two edges share the same label, they are meant to be joined together — glued, taped, or sewn — during construction. Work through the labels in order as you assemble: find the matching pair, align the edges, and secure them. This system means you never have to guess which pieces connect to which.
</Accordion>

Piece labels (the overall part names or numbers) also appear on each piece so you can quickly identify which part of the costume a piece belongs to, especially useful when patterns from multiple parts share the same page.

## Managing Pages

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Adding Pages" icon="plus" href="/patterns/pattern-layout">
    Add a new page using the page management panel when your pieces exceed the current page space. Each new page represents another physical sheet of paper.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Setting Paper Size" icon="file" href="/patterns/printing">
    Set the paper size for all pages in the layout settings. Choose from standard sizes like **A4**, **Letter**, or **Tabloid** to match the paper you plan to print on. Make sure this matches your printer's actual paper size before printing.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Once your pieces are arranged to your satisfaction, you are ready to move on to printing.
