This exercise makes two separate foam halves that share an editable outline and one assembled heat form. Start with a simple center edge before trying curved glue joints.

## 1. Draw and promote the first half

In **Create 2D**, choose **Line**. Draw a closed half-panel with a straight center segment and a rounded outer shoulder profile. Keep the outline on one side of its center edge. Use **Ctrl** to place curve anchors, then refine the outline with right-click **Edit Shape**.

Right-click the closed outline and choose **Promote to foam pattern**. Set **Thickness** to **6 mm** and start with **Form → Flat**.

**Checkpoint:** one flat cutting outline and one thick foam half appear in the Patterns workspace.

## 2. Create its mirrored partner

Select the half and choose **Create mirror instance**. The second piece starts beside it. Move a point on one half and confirm that its mirrored partner changes too. Undo the test edit if needed.

Keep the cutting outlines apart on the sheet. You do not need to overlap them to assemble the shoulder.

**Checkpoint:** two selectable pieces have matching reflected shapes and separate physical identities.

## 3. Glue the center edges

Click **Glue**. In the 2D view, click the center segment of the first half, then the corresponding segment of the mirror. Press **Escape** to finish.

The 3D preview aligns the halves at their center edges. Their paper positions remain unchanged. If the tool reports different lengths, check the pieces' scales and select corresponding segments.

## 4. Heat form the whole shoulder

Select either half and open **Form → Spherical form**. Start with **Gentle curve**. The recommended radius uses the size and thickness of the combined assembly.

Reduce the radius gradually for a deeper shoulder shape. Both halves use one sphere center and direction. Open **Foam forming properties** to adjust piece stiffness and assembly heat softness.

Read **Foam preview status** for the remaining glue error and maximum edge stretch. A deeper dome asks more of the flat pattern. You can adjust the outline, forming radius, or glue edge as you refine the design.

**Checkpoint:** the halves form one shoulder bell while remaining two separate cutting patterns.

## 5. Add raised details

Select the half that will support a detail and open **Layers**. Use it as the base, add a layer, and add smaller promoted patterns. Move those patterns with **Move on surface**. Assign each its own material or apply one material to its whole layer.

Use **Details** to place dome rivets, eyelets, or washers on either half. Change the shoulder's sphere radius and check that the layers and details follow their host surfaces.

## 6. Position and copy the shoulder

Use **Move foam** or **Place** to position the assembled shoulder in the left-hand 3D view. Clicking either half moves the assembly. Enable **Attach as part** when you want it in the main Costume view.

Select the assembly's original base/root pattern and choose **Create mirror instance** to make the opposite shoulder. Its pieces and glue connections are copied and mirrored. Corresponding outlines remain linked, while the new shoulder keeps independent forming, placement, and materials.

Use ordinary copy/paste or **Duplicate independently** when the second shoulder should have its own geometry. The copied halves retain their links to each other.

## 7. Arrange the cutting sheet

Move and rotate the 2D outlines into a printable layout. This does not rearrange the glued shoulder in 3D. Each half remains a separate template.

Continue with [Print patterns](/patterns/printing). Review [Instances and gluing](/foam-preview/instances-and-gluing) for making individual pieces unique and repairing glue connections.
