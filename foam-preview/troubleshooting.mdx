## The foam tools are missing

This section covers an unreleased preview. Use a build that includes the new spline foam tools. Older spline objects may use the legacy editing tools. Start a new pattern with **Draw foam** to use the new system.

## The 3D foam preview is missing

Check that **Foam** is enabled and that the pattern and its parent layers are visible. The outline must be closed, enclose an area, and have no self-intersections. Open paths remain drawing lines.

Select the pattern and read **Foam preview status** in its properties. The status explains invalid geometry or settings. Check thickness, sphere radius, and edge depth.

For a layered pattern, enable the base's preview too. A layer depends on the base surface.

The left-hand **Patterns** view shows unattached foam. Enabling **Foam**, changing thickness or forming, or choosing **Use selected base** frames the preview. Use the view's fit controls if you have moved the camera away.

In the main **Costume** view, foam appears only after **Attach as part** is enabled on its base pattern. Setting a layer base alone does not attach it.

## The piece disappears after spherical forming

Open **Form → Spherical form**, click **Gentle curve**, and confirm. The recommended radius scales with the pattern. A smaller radius makes a deeper dome; it must stay above the minimum displayed for this outline and thickness. An older saved radius may be too small after resizing an outline or increasing thickness.

If the preview is still missing, read **Foam preview status**. Check that the outline does not cross itself and undo the last outline edit if needed.

## The sphere curves inward

Select the piece and choose **Form → Reverse forming direction**. This changes the whole base stack, including its layers and attached details. New spherical forms start with an outward dome, but an existing saved sphere keeps its previous direction.

## The preview looks too dense or angular

Each foam pattern's complete solid uses at most **2,000 triangles**. Curvature and edge finishes share that budget; the 2D cutting outline keeps its full precision. Curved faces use smooth shading, while side walls retain a distinct edge. Tighter spherical curvature may show more visible facets within the same budget. Try **Gentle curve** for a subtle form.

If **Foam preview status** reports too many corners for the preview, simplify an unusually complex outline. The preview keeps concave corners to avoid bridging across cutouts.

## A pattern will not fit in a layer

The whole outline must fit inside the base outline. Check corners and segments near concave indentations. Having all corners inside is not enough if a segment crosses a cutout.

Make the pattern smaller or reshape it, then add it again. A pattern with its own foam layers cannot become a member of another stack in this preview.

## The layer looks like one flat plate

Orbit to the raised side, or use the front-view control. Looking at the back of the base can hide the pieces on top. The first layer rests above the base automatically; keep **Layer gap** at **0 mm** for glued pieces. A different material color on an upper pattern can make the composition easier to see.

The 2D view shows the cutting-sheet layout, not an assembled overlay. Use **Move on surface** in the 3D view to reposition a layer member. Translating or rotating its template in 2D leaves that assembled placement unchanged.

## Clicking a foam plate does not move it

Click **Move foam**, then left-drag a visible plate in the left-hand Patterns view. Release modifier keys first. Locked layers and frozen patterns cannot be moved. A small click selects the base without moving it; drag farther to start movement.

The move follows the camera's viewing plane. Orbit to a side view to change depth, or enter exact coordinates with **Place**. Finish **Details** placement or **Move on surface** before arranging bases. Press **Escape** to cancel an unfinished drag.

## A layered pattern disappears after a 2D edit

The new outline or transform may extend outside the base. Surface dragging prevents out-of-bounds moves, but other editing controls can create an invalid arrangement. Undo that change or bring the outline back inside the base. Read **Foam preview status** for the affected pattern.

## An upper layer floats above a gap

Each layer starts above the tallest pattern in the lower layers. It shares one elevation across the base surface. Reduce **Set layer gap**, adjust thickness, or change layer order. The preview does not place upper pieces into empty spaces between lower patterns.

Hiding a lower layer does not remove its thickness from the stack. Remove it from the composition if you want to change stack height.

## Changing a layer member's Form changes the whole stack

That is the shared forming workflow. **Form** and **Place** route to the base pattern. Thickness, Edge Style, and material remain individual pattern settings.

## Edge depth becomes invalid after a thickness change

A fixed millimeter depth can exceed the new foam thickness. The **Thickness** dialog explains this and keeps the previous thickness. Lower the depth or use a percentage, then change thickness again. Editing properties directly can still create an invalid combination; correct the depth to restore the preview.

## A material changed every pattern in a layer

**Apply material to whole layer** updates all current members and the default for later additions. Undo to restore the prior assignments. Use **Set pattern material** on a pattern row for a single-piece change.

## A detail cannot be placed or moved

Use **Place on surface** or **Move details** in the left-hand Patterns preview, or in the Costume view after attachment. Click a visible foam front or back face. Side walls do not accept placement. In the Costume view, a visible costume mesh in front of the foam blocks clicking through to it.

Details stay on the same owner and face during a drag. To put a detail on another pattern, remove it and place a new copy there.

## A detail disappeared after an outline edit

Its stored anchor may now be outside the foam outline. Undo the edit or adjust the detail's flat surface anchor to place it inside again. Removing the owner also removes the detail from the visible composition; undo restores the attachment.

## An eyelet has no hole through the foam

The detail model has its own opening. It does not subtract material from the foam. Plan and mark the physical hole separately.

## The real foam does not match the spherical preview

The preview shows a geometric forming shape. It does not calculate stretching, heating, glue, or springback. Test a physical sample and adjust the flat cut pattern and forming radius for your foam and construction method.
