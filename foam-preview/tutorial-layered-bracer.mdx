Build a curved bracer with a 6 mm base, two raised 2 mm details in one layer, and decorative dome rivets. This exercise uses the unreleased foam tools and keeps the flat cut outlines available throughout.

The sizes below are practice settings. Fit the finished design to your costume before cutting foam.

## 1. Draw the base

Open a workspace and choose the **Patterns** tab. Click **Draw foam**. Use the 2D drawing view on the right and the 3D foam preview on the left.

In the 2D view, click four anchors to draw a broad rectangle or tapered bracer plate. Make it large enough to leave room for two smaller details. Click near the first anchor to close it.

Select the new pattern. Click **Thickness**, enter **6 mm**, and confirm. Check that the solid foam preview appears in the 3D view. Toggle **Foam** if it is hidden.

**Checkpoint:** you have a flat outline in 2D and a solid 6 mm piece in 3D.

## 2. Refine the outline

Click **Edit points**. Select a corner and move it to adjust the taper.

Try **Curve** on one segment, then drag its handles to soften that side. If you need another anchor, select the segment's starting anchor and use **Divide** with one new anchor. The curve keeps its shape until you edit the new point.

Leave point editing with **Edit points**. Keep the outline closed and avoid crossing its edges.

## 3. Form and place the bracer

Open **Form**, choose **Horizontal bend**, and enter **90°**. This bends across the pattern's flat X direction.

Orbit the left-hand 3D preview to inspect the bend. Adjust the angle as needed. Use **Reverse forming direction** if it curves the wrong way. You will attach the completed stack to the costume in step 9.

Briefly try **Vertical bend** to see the other axis, then return to horizontal. You can also try **Spherical form** and click **Gentle curve** to see forming in two directions. Increase the radius for a flatter piece, or decrease it toward the displayed minimum for a deeper dome. Return to the mode you want for your bracer.

**Checkpoint:** the formed piece changes in 3D while its flat cut outline stays the same.

## 4. Give the base an angle cut

Select the base and click **Edge Style**. Choose **Angle cut — 45°**, **50%**, then confirm.

For 6 mm foam, this removes 3 mm down from the top edge and 3 mm inward. Open the dialog again and switch to **Millimeters**. It should read **3 mm**. Switch back to percent if you want the cut to follow later thickness changes.

Inspect the edge from the side in 3D. The bottom cut outline remains intact.

## 5. Create a layer with two patterns

Select the base, click **Layers**, then click **Use selected base**. Click **Add layer** and single-click its row.

Return to **Draw foam** and create a smaller closed raised plate. Give it **2 mm** thickness. Create a second small pattern beside it. To make a rounded badge, place curve anchors with **Ctrl** or refine a small outline with **Smooth** and its handles.

Select both smaller patterns in the 2D view. Keep the destination layer row selected in **Foam layers**, then click **Add selected patterns**.

If a shape is rejected, make it smaller or change its outline so it fits within the base. The tool attempts to center an out-of-bounds shape before rejecting it.

**Checkpoint:** one layer contains two separate patterns, and both follow the base bend.

## 6. Slide a detail across the bend

Click **Move on surface**. In the 3D view, click and drag one of the raised foam patterns.

Move it across the curved bracer and toward the outer edge. It follows the bend and stays within the base outline. Release to keep the move. Try undo, then redo, to check its position.

Click **Finish moving**. Double-click the raised pattern's row to select it. Give it a **Rounded** Edge Style with **50%** depth. On 2 mm foam, that is a 1 mm radius.

## 7. Color the layer, then override one pattern

Single-click the layer row. Choose a material, or **Custom color...**, then click **Apply material to whole layer**. For this exercise, use a dark metallic color or a dark gray.

Click one raised pattern's row. Choose a contrasting material or color, then click **Set pattern material**. Only that pattern changes.

Double-click the base row, then use **Form** to change the bend slightly. The colored raised patterns still share the same forming.

**Checkpoint:** the layer has a common starting material, with one independent pattern override.

## 8. Place decorative rivets

Click **Details**. Choose a **6 mm** or **8 mm Dome rivet**, then click **Place on surface**.

Click on the base and on a raised pattern to place several rivets. Each rivet takes the material of the piece you clicked. Keep them away from the outer edge for this exercise.

Click **Move details** and drag one rivet along its current surface. Click **Finish** when you are done.

Change the base bend once more. The rivets remain attached. Their own dimensions stay fixed while their position and orientation follow the surface.

## 9. Review, save, and prepare the cut patterns

Use layer checkboxes to inspect the base and raised shapes separately. Check the stack from several camera angles.

Select the base and click **Attach as part**. Open the **Costume** tab and use **Place** to position and rotate the stack near the forearm. Check its fit against the avatar, then save the project. The whole stack shares the base placement. You can turn **Attach as part** off to keep it only in the Patterns preview.

Review the flat patterns in 2D. Forming, edge finishes, and materials do not replace the outlines you cut. Decorative hardware does not automatically add drilling marks.

Continue with [Print patterns](/patterns/printing). Print a test page, verify its scale with a ruler, and label the foam thickness, edge depth, and hardware positions for your physical build.

If something does not match a checkpoint, use [Foam troubleshooting](/foam-preview/troubleshooting).
