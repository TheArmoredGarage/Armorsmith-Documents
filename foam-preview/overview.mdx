Start with the shape you want to cut from a flat sheet of foam. Draw its outline in 2D, give it a thickness, then form and position the resulting piece in the 3D costume view. Keep editing the same outline as you refine the design.

## Work from the cut pattern to the costume

1. **Draw foam** creates the flat outline.
2. **Edit points** refines anchors, handles, and individual segments.
3. **Thickness** gives the piece its physical depth.
4. **Form** previews horizontal bending, vertical bending, or forming over a sphere.
5. **Place** positions and rotates the piece in 3D.
6. **Layers** adds smaller patterns that follow the same forming.
7. **Edge Style** previews angle cuts or rounded top edges.
8. **Details** attaches decorative dome rivets, eyelets, and washers.

The 2D outline remains your flat cut template. Forming, edge finishes, materials, and decorative hardware change the 3D preview without replacing that outline.

## Understand the preview

Foam forming is a geometric design preview. Use it to judge placement, layering, and appearance. It does not predict heat, foam stretching, glue behavior, or springback. Spherical forming changes distances across the formed surface; test your physical cut pattern before committing to a finished build.

This preview includes the drawing, editing, forming, layering, edge, and detail tools described here. Offset outlines, trim/extend, join/split, persistent symmetry constraints, automatic seam allowances, darts, and notches are outside this documented preview. Existing tools for other pattern types have their own behavior.

For paper layout and print scale, continue with [Print patterns](/patterns/printing).
