Armorsmith Designer lets you assign visual materials — colors and basic shaders — to individual parts or groups in your scene. Materials serve one purpose in Armorsmith: making your costume easier to read and navigate in the 3D viewport. A helmet painted red and a chest plate painted blue are instantly distinguishable at a glance, which speeds up selection and reduces mistakes when your scene contains many parts.

## What Materials Do (and Don't Do)

Materials in Armorsmith are **viewport-only visuals**. They help you organize and differentiate parts during the design process. They have no influence on the flat patterns or templates that Armorsmith generates — your printed output will look the same regardless of what material color you've assigned.

<Note>
  Material settings do **not** affect exported templates or printed patterns. Colors and shaders are purely for visual organization inside Armorsmith Designer. If you need to indicate material types or colors on your physical build, add notes to your patterns separately.
</Note>

## Assigning a Material to a Part

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select the part">
    Click the part in the 3D viewport or in the Parts panel to select it. You can also select a group to apply a material to all parts within it at once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Property Grid">
    The **Property Grid** panel appears on the right side of the interface by default. If it's not visible, go to **View → Property Grid** to show it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the Material section">
    In the Property Grid, scroll to the **Material** section. You'll see fields for color, shader type, and opacity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a color or shader">
    Click the color swatch to open the color picker and choose a fill color. Optionally adjust the shader or opacity settings for a different visual style.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    The part updates in the viewport immediately. No extra save step is needed — material assignments are stored as part of your project file.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Color-Coding Your Costume

One practical approach is to assign a consistent color to each major section of the costume. For example:

| Color | Section |
|-------|---------|
| Red | Helmet |
| Blue | Chest plate |
| Orange | Back plate |
| Green | Shoulder pads |
| Purple | Arms and gauntlets |
| Yellow | Legs and boots |

This makes it easy to tell sections apart when rotating around a complex scene, and helps when discussing the design with collaborators — "the green group" is faster to communicate than "the third part from the top in the left panel."

<Tip>
  Apply materials as you import and position each part rather than waiting until the end. Color-coding from the start makes it much easier to select the right part in a dense scene, especially once you have 30 or more pieces in the viewport.
</Tip>
