Attaching a part to your avatar is the core step that makes Armorsmith's pattern generation meaningful. When a part is properly positioned against the avatar, the app knows exactly where it sits on your body — which means any templates or flat patterns it generates will be sized and shaped to fit you. Loose, unanchored parts floating in the scene won't produce accurate results, so take the time to fit each piece before moving on.

## How Attaching Works

After you import a model, it lands in the scene at a default position. "Attaching" simply means using the Move, Rotate, and Scale tools to bring that part into the correct location and orientation relative to the avatar's body. There is no automatic snap-to-body button — positioning is a manual process that gives you full control over how each piece sits.

## Positioning a Part on the Avatar

<Steps>
  <Step title="Import your model">
    Go to **File → Import**, select your file, and confirm the import. The part appears in the viewport. If it's the wrong size, correct the scale now before positioning. See [Move, Rotate, and Scale Costume Parts](/parts/transforming-parts) for guidance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the part">
    Click the part in the 3D viewport or in the Parts panel to select it. The transform gizmo will appear on the part.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Move the part into position">
    Use the **Move** tool (default shortcut: **W**) to drag the part toward the correct region of the avatar — helmet to the head, chest plate to the torso, and so on. Drag the red (X), green (Y), or blue (Z) axis arrows on the gizmo to move along a single axis at a time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rotate to match the avatar's contours">
    Switch to the **Rotate** tool (shortcut: **E**) and drag the ring handles to adjust the part's orientation. Most parts will need at least a small rotation to lie flush against the body.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scale if necessary">
    If the part is still the wrong size after import-time corrections, use the **Scale** tool (shortcut: **R**) to resize it. Scale uniformly from the center handle, or per-axis using the individual handles.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check fit from all angles">
    Rotate the viewport (middle-mouse drag or the view cube) to inspect the part from the front, side, and back. Make sure it sits on the surface of the avatar without clipping through or floating too far away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tips for a Good Fit

- **Keep the avatar in T-pose.** The default T-pose gives you symmetrical reference points and makes it easier to judge alignment for paired parts like shoulder pads or greaves.
- **Check all angles.** A part may look correct from the front but clip through the body when viewed from the side. Always do a full 360° inspection.
- **Use the Measure tool for clearance.** For parts that need a gap between the costume and the body (for foam padding, for example), use the Measure tool to verify the distance. See [Group Parts and Measure Distances](/parts/grouping-and-measuring) for instructions.

<Tip>
  Work through your costume one region at a time rather than importing everything at once. A good sequence is: helmet → chest → back → shoulders → arms → legs. Finishing and checking each section before moving to the next keeps the scene manageable and makes it easier to spot fit problems early.
</Tip>
