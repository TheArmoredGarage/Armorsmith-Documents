Your avatar is the body you fit costume parts around. Start with the built-in body closest to your proportions, enter your measurements, and refine the silhouette before attaching parts.

Open **Avatar → Edit Avatar** to show **Avatar Customization**. Changes appear immediately in the **Costume viewport**. Expand **Full Body**, **Measurements**, **Circumferences**, and **Details** as you work.

<Frame>
  ![Avatar Customization panel beside an avatar with measurement guides](/images/Avatar-Customization.png)
</Frame>

## Before you start

Have a flexible tape measure, your recorded measurements, and the clothing you expect to wear beneath the costume. Set your preferred measurement units in [**Settings → Preferences**](/interface/preferences).

For a costume worn over a bodysuit or supportive undergarment, measure while wearing it. Keep a separate set of measurements if you also need an avatar for a different clothing setup.

## Create a built-in avatar

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the base body">
    Under **Full Body**, choose **Male** or **Female** in **Gender**. Use the body whose starting shape best suits the person you are fitting.

    Choose this before entering measurements: changing **Gender** resets measurements and customizations to the selected body's defaults.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the overall height">
    Set **Height Estimate** first. Use its slider or enter a measurement in the value field and press **Enter**.

    Height scales the avatar and its existing dimensions together. Set it before refining individual lengths and circumferences.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match the body lengths">
    Expand **Measurements**. Start with **Torso Length**, **Arm Length**, and **Inseam**, then refine the individual segments.

    Hover over a measurement to highlight its guide in orange. Use the endpoints to identify where to measure your body. Enter the value and press **Enter**.

    [Body measurements](/avatar/body-measurements) explains the fields and how compound measurements affect individual segments.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set circumferences and shape">
    Expand **Circumferences**. Enter measurements around your head, torso, arms, and legs at the levels shown by the guides.

    **Size** adjusts the circumference setting. **Shape** adjusts the balance between width and depth. Compare front and side views as you refine the shape.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Refine the silhouette">
    Expand **Details**. Use **Head** for facial features and **Torso** for breast size, height, projection, and butt size.

    The three breast controls have neutral midpoints and work independently. Follow [Chest and breast shape](/avatar/chest-and-breast-shape) to adjust them without using ribcage size to compensate for breast fullness.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check and save">
    Inspect the avatar from the front, side, and back. Recheck the numbers after changing height or compound lengths.

    Choose **Avatar → Save As** and save an `.avatar` file with a descriptive name. See [Saving and reusing avatars](/avatar/saving-and-reusing-avatars).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Understand the editor sections

| Section | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **Full Body** | Base body, height, hair, colors, visibility, and a reference image. |
| **Measurements** | Body lengths and the positions of joints and body segments. |
| **Circumferences** | Dimensions around the body and the width/depth balance of those regions. |
| **Details** | Local shape adjustments for the face and torso. These sliders are shape controls rather than tape-measure values. |

## Use a reference photograph

Under **Full Body**, enable **Show Reference Image** and choose a JPG or PNG. Use **Reference Size** to scale the displayed image and **Reference Offset** to move it vertically.

Use a photograph taken straight on, with the whole body visible and the camera level. A side photograph helps you check depth. Wide-angle distortion, a tilted camera, and loose clothing can make a photograph misleading.

Treat the image as a visual reference. Loading it does not automatically extract measurements or resize the avatar.

## Check the avatar before fitting parts

- Hide attached parts with **Show Costume** to see the body clearly.
- Use [viewport navigation](/interface/viewport-navigation) to inspect the front, side, and back.
- Check shoulder and hip width, torso depth, limb lengths, and the position of the fullest chest area.
- Check the intended clothing setup, including footwear, hair, padding, and supportive undergarments.

An avatar is a fitting reference. Its shape does not guarantee that an imported costume part fits. [Attach the part](/parts/attaching-parts), adjust its position and scale, and use [Fit Checker](/parts/fit-checker) to inspect clearance before generating final patterns.

## Reset a measurement or start again

Use the **Reset** button beside a length or circumference to restore that field's default. Recheck related values after resetting a compound measurement.

**Avatar → Reset Avatar** restores the avatar's defaults, including measurements and appearance settings. Save a copy before using it. To discard only pose changes, use **Avatar → Reset Pose** instead.

For a scan or a model made in another application, follow [Custom avatars](/avatar/custom-avatars). For a shape that still does not look right, see [Avatar troubleshooting](/avatar/avatar-troubleshooting).
